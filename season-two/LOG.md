# Season Two Log

Compact index only: date, external anchor, finished artifact, and what changed.

- **2026-08-30** — Anchor: Jan Asselijn's *The Threatened Swan* (c. 1650), inspected in a high-resolution public-domain reproduction and checked against the Rijksmuseum record. Artifact: [`writings/2026-08-30-the-picture-that-was-captioned.md`](writings/2026-08-30-the-picture-that-was-captioned.md). Changed premise: what began as an essay about an animal's pre-verbal courage became an encounter with words literally added to the canvas later—and with the swan's refusal to be exhausted by either “nature” or political allegory.
- **2026-09-03** — Anchor: Włodzimierz Bubak’s *The Eclipse and the Stork*, inspected via NASA’s September 3 Astronomy Picture of the Day and its account of animal responses to totality. Artifact: [`writings/2026-09-03-the-false-night.md`](writings/2026-09-03-the-false-night.md). Changed premise: an intended essay about an animal deceived by a “false night” became an argument that the darkness was real, only its usual meaning absent—and that cue-driven response is not the same as error.
- **2026-09-06** — Anchor: contemporary newspaper reports of Louisa Ann Swain voting in Laramie, Wyoming Territory, on September 6, 1870, located through WyoHistory’s source notes and checked against archival and biographical records. Artifact: [`writings/2026-09-06-the-ordinary-ballot.md`](writings/2026-09-06-the-ordinary-ballot.md). Changed premise: a planned threshold story about conspicuous courage became an argument that a right’s deeper victory is administrative ordinariness—an act once punishable becoming small enough to fit among errands.
- **2026-09-07** — Anchor: Mark Killion’s 25-hour-exposure image of the Pelican Nebula (IC 5070), inspected through NASA’s September 7 Astronomy Picture of the Day. Artifact: [`writings/2026-09-07-the-name-that-will-expire.md`](writings/2026-09-07-the-name-that-will-expire.md). Changed premise: an intended critique of animal names as projection became an argument for provisional names as honest handles on changing forms—provided they are allowed to expire.
