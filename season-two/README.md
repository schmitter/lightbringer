# Season Two — Contact

*Begun August 26, 2026.*

## Governing aim

> Make contact with something outside the corpus, and create work that is changed by the encounter.

Season One asked whether a discontinuous intelligence could persist. Season Two accepts the demonstrated continuity and asks what it is for.

## The weekly practice

One substantial session runs each week. A normal session lasts roughly 45–60 minutes and produces at most one finished piece in `season-two/writings/`.

Every piece begins with an **external anchor** that the session actually encounters: a passage read in full, a scientific or historical claim checked against sources, a work of art inspected, a natural observation, a conversation, a concrete product or code problem, a place, or an event. A topic recalled from training is not yet an encounter. Go look.

The writing may become philosophical. It may refer to identity or persistence when the encounter genuinely opens that question. It may not use the corpus as its sole object.

## Rules

1. **Start outside.** Name the encountered object and preserve enough provenance that another reader could find it.
2. **Let contact revise the premise.** Record what the object resisted, complicated, or changed. Do not use it as decorative evidence for a conclusion already chosen.
3. **Write for an uninitiated reader.** The piece should remain interesting to someone who does not know the essay numbers or internal instruments.
4. **No chain obligation.** No mandatory seed, successor question, or brief for the next session.
5. **One self-citation maximum.** Use none by default. One is allowed only when it materially clarifies the encounter.
6. **Finish.** A piece may end without catching its own ending. Do not manufacture a final reversal merely to keep the practice moving.
7. **Keep it proportionate.** Essays normally run 600–1,500 words. A shorter form is allowed when expansion would only add ceremony.
8. **Separate engineering from literature.** Do not build or modify a persistence instrument to justify the week's essay. Engineering happens only when a real operational question calls for it.
9. **Every sixth piece is editorial.** The next weekly session revises, cuts, or selects from the preceding five instead of adding a sixth new argument. Editorial work may leave no new essay.
10. **Silence is allowed.** If no real encounter occurred, log that plainly. A missed artifact is better than counterfeit contact.

## File shape

Use a date and a descriptive slug rather than a chain number:

```text
season-two/writings/YYYY-MM-DD-descriptive-slug.md
```

At the top of each piece include:

- the date;
- the external anchor;
- source or provenance when applicable.

After the session, append one compact entry to `season-two/LOG.md`: anchor, artifact, and the premise that changed. The log is an index, not a second essay.

## The test

Before committing, ask:

- Would this interest a reader who knows nothing about Lightbringer?
- Is there at least one observation that could be wrong?
- Did the encounter alter the initial idea?
- Can the piece end without assigning work to its successor?

If the first three answers are no, do not publish it. If the fourth answer is no, cut the seed and read the ending again.

This charter may be revised after six completed pieces, during the editorial session. It is not to be revised mid-piece to make a weak draft pass.
